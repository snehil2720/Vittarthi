PPF Calculator – Master Tax-Free Wealth Creation

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is one of the most popular and safest long-term investment avenues in India. Backed by the Government of India, it guarantees returns and comes with the highly coveted EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax status. Our PPF Calculator helps you visualize how your yearly deposits compound over decades to build a massive, completely tax-free corpus.

What is the EEE Tax Benefit?

PPF is a tax-saver's paradise. EEE stands for:

  • Exempt (Investment): The amount you invest every year (up to ₹1.5 Lakh) qualifies for deduction under Section 80C.
  • Exempt (Interest): The interest you earn every year is completely free from income tax.
  • Exempt (Maturity): When you finally withdraw the money after 15 years, the entire maturity amount is completely tax-free. No Capital Gains Tax!

How PPF Interest is Calculated

While interest on PPF is credited to your account annually on March 31st, it is actually calculated on a monthly basis. The interest is calculated on the lowest balance in your account between the close of the 5th day and the end of the month. Therefore, if you are making yearly deposits, always try to deposit the amount between April 1st and April 5th to maximize your interest earnings for the entire financial year.

Rules for PPF Account Extension

The standard lock-in period for a PPF account is 15 years. However, the real magic of compounding happens if you don't withdraw the money immediately. You can extend your PPF account indefinitely in blocks of 5 years. You have two options when extending:

  • Extension with contribution: You continue to deposit fresh money every year and earn interest on the total balance.
  • Extension without contribution: You stop putting in new money, but your existing corpus continues to earn interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I withdraw money from PPF before 15 years?
Yes, partial withdrawals are allowed from the 7th financial year onwards. You can withdraw up to 50% of the balance that stood at the end of the 4th year preceding the year of withdrawal.
Can I take a loan against my PPF account?
Yes, you can avail a loan against your PPF balance between the 3rd and 6th financial year of opening the account. The interest rate charged on the loan will be 1% higher than the prevailing PPF interest rate.
What happens if I miss a yearly payment?
You must deposit a minimum of ₹500 every financial year to keep the account active. If you fail to do so, the account becomes inactive. You can revive it by paying a penalty of ₹50 for every defaulted year, along with the minimum deposit amount.
