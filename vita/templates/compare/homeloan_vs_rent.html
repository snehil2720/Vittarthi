Frequently Asked Questions

India's most common questions about buying vs renting

It depends on your city, tenure, and goals. In expensive metros like Mumbai, renting is cheaper short-term. But over 15–20 years, buying typically builds more net worth through property appreciation and equity. If you plan to stay in the same city for 10+ years, buying is generally the better long-term financial decision.

The break-even is when the net worth from buying (property value minus remaining loan) equals the renter's net worth (investments minus rent paid). On average in India, this is 8–12 years. In expensive cities like Mumbai, it can be 12–15 years. Our calculator shows your exact break-even year based on your inputs.

Mathematically, if equity SIP returns (12%+) exceed home loan rate (8.5%), investing wins in pure returns. But home buying offers emotional security, inflation hedge, forced savings discipline, and tax benefits under 24b and 80C. Most advisors recommend buying if you plan to stay 10+ years in the same city.

Home Loan: Section 24b (₹2L interest deduction) + Section 80C (₹1.5L principal) + Section 80EEA for first-time buyers (additional ₹1.5L) = up to ₹1.56 lakh saved per year at 30% slab. Rent: HRA exemption (minimum of actual HRA, 50%/40% of salary, rent minus 10% salary). Note: New tax regime (default from FY2024-25) does not allow HRA deduction.

In Mumbai and Delhi, rent-to-EMI ratio is 1:2.5 or more — meaning EMI is 2.5× the rent for the same flat. This makes renting attractive in the short term. However, Mumbai property has appreciated 8–10% annually over 20 years. If you can afford the higher EMI and plan to stay 15+ years, buying is generally worth it even in expensive cities. The key is affordability — EMI should be less than 40% of your monthly income.
